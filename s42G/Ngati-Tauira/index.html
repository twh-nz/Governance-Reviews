The Charities Register record, the Trust Deed, the Board Charter, 2 board packs, the plans, 6 policies, the Risk Register and a Procedures Survey confirmed by the Chair. Full list on page 6.

Tūtohu Ture  |  Charities Act Review (s42G)

The Act asks the Board to consider 3 questions. Here is what the procedures show for each.

Question 1Yes, with actions

Are the Trust’s governance procedures fit for purpose?

In place. The Trust Deed is thorough and grounded in kaupapa Māori. The Board Charter covers compliance well. Board packs are ready for decisions, and a committee watches over money and risk.

To address. Four procedures are missing: written delegated authorities for the Chair, a Board Secretary role, a succession plan with a deputy Chair, and a skills matrix. Induction stops after a new trustee’s first hui. Matters 1, 2 and 5 on page 5 close these gaps.

Question 2Not yet

Do the procedures assist the Trust to achieve its charitable purpose?

In place. The Board shares its kaupapa and its direction. The Strategic Plan names the people the Trust serves, and the Operational Plan is reported on. The Trust delivers its programmes well.

To address. The Strategic Plan has no measures, so the Board cannot see from its papers whether the strategy is working. The Plan was not tabled at either hui we reviewed, and the Annual Calendar is blank. Matter 3 fixes this. Once measures are reported to the Board, it can record a yes.

Question 3Yes, with actions

Do the procedures assist the Trust to comply with the Charities Act?

In place. Financial controls, the external audit, the conflicts of interest register and the Deed’s rules for the AGM are all in place and used. Annual Returns and officer certifications are filed on time.

To address. Nobody holds written responsibility for records and filings. There is no schedule for reviewing policies. The Trust Deed has no review date and no rule for very large transactions. Matters 2, 4 and 5 address these.

Ngā Mana Pakari  |  Strengths

The procedures the Board should keep while it builds the rest.

  • Every trustee puts the kaupapa first. The Strategic Plan names marae and iwi members as the people the Trust serves, and the registered purpose still matches it.
  • Financial reporting to the Board is strong and ready for decisions. Every pack carries budget against actual and the balance sheet, and the FY2025 audit is signed.
  • The board pack is much stronger than it was a year ago. Each pack now has timed items, recorded resolutions, an interests register and an action list.
  • The Trust Deed is a thorough founding document grounded in kaupapa Māori. 25 of the 33 items we check in a trust deed are in place.

Ngā Take Matua  |  Matters to Address

Five matters to address are listed below, in order of recommended priority.

1

Write down what the Chair and the Manager may each decide alone

Two short lists of delegated authorities, approved by the Board and added to the Delegations policy. Owner: the Chair with the Manager. By the next board hui.

Can follow the resolution.

2

Define the Board Secretary role, including records and filings

A one-page position description naming who keeps the minutes and registers up to date and who files the Annual Return. Owner: the Chair. By the next board hui.

Can follow the resolution.

3

Agree measures for the Strategic Plan and fill in the Annual Calendar

No more than 6 measures, reported in every board pack. A filled calendar showing when the Plan, the Risk Register, the budget and each policy come to the Board. Owner: the Board, led by the Chair. By: the first hui of 2027.

Needed before the Board can record a yes to Question 2.

4

Add a review date and a large-transaction rule to the Trust Deed

A clause that sets when the Deed is next reviewed, and a clause that names the size of transaction that needs a full Board decision. This is legal drafting. Owner: the Chair, with the Trust’s lawyer. Lodge the amended Deed with Charities Services.

Can follow the resolution.

5

Set a policy review schedule, and table the Risk Register twice a year

A table of every policy with its owner and review date, kept with the Annual Calendar. The Risk Register comes to the Board at 2 named hui a year. Owner: the Board Secretary. By March 2027.

Can follow the resolution.

What this review did not look at

This review looked at what your Board has and uses. It did not look at how your Board works together, how it relates to management and to whānau, or how trustees experience governing. Those are the questions the Governance Aromatawai answers, with every trustee heard. The fee for this review is credited in full against an Aromatawai started within 12 months.
